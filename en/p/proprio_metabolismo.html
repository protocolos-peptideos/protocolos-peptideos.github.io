!

Experimental material. Read before using anything from here.

Nothing on this page is a medical recommendation, prescription or treatment plan. It is an organized translation of protocols circulating in research communities and, where it exists, of what published trials tested. The two are marked differently — and they are not equivalent.

Most of the compounds here have no FDA approval for human use. Several are sold labeled "research use only", which means they have not gone through purity, sterility or dosage controls for human consumption. A community-reported dose is not a validated dose: it is what someone reported having done.

Talk to a licensed health professional before considering any of these compounds. If you already use one and feel anything unexpected, seek care — do not wait for the next routine test.

Specific caution for this compound: None of these compounds has an active drug registration in Brazil. Exenatide is a GLP-1 agonist with the same precautions as the class; AICAR is a lab tool; CBL-514 has not yet finished phase 3. What is bought under these names is sold as a research reagent, with no control over purity, sterility or dose.

Summary

Six weight and metabolism compounds that were missing from the site: exenatide, oxyntomodulin, efinopegdutide, AICAR (acadesine), CBL-514 and L-carnitine. They range from the most studied to almost nothing, and what separates them is always the same question: in whom was it tested, and for what.

Six names from outside the pen lineup

The site already has semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, survodutide, cagrilintide, mazdutide and the next-generation incretins page. Left out were exenatide, approved in the United States in 2005; oxyntomodulin, a gut hormone; efinopegdutide, from MSD; AICAR, which the store I took the list from describes as an exercise mimetic; CBL-514, an injection for localized fat; and L-carnitine.

Three method caveats. One: AICAR OR acadesine returns the entire literature of a lab tool, and the number is a ceiling. To measure that: (AICAR OR acadesine) AND AMPK returns 2,164 of the 2,891 articles. Two: the L-carnitine search returns records in which it enters as co-treatment in trials of something else; the largest phase 3 is an antiviral trial in HIV. Three: oxyntomodulin is a hormone the body produces, and some of the records measure it in the blood.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • Exenatide has 93 phase 3 trials and no active registration. The largest is EXSCEL, a cardiovascular outcome trial, with 14,752 participants. In the United States, the search returns BYETTA as discontinued and a synthetic generic approved in 2024. It is the one with the most phase 3 trials on this page — and it has no product registered in Brazil.
  • Oxyntomodulin does not have a single phase 3 trial, across 476 articles. The largest record is phase 2, from OPKO Health, Inc., with 420 participants in type 2 diabetes; the rest is phase 1 or physiology — the hormone measured after bariatric surgery, after exercise, after a meal.
  • Efinopegdutide is, so far, in phase 2: 4 phase 2 records, all from MSD and all in fatty liver, and none in phase 3.
  • AICAR's only phase 3 trial was terminated. Under the pharmaceutical name acadesine, it was tested in 3,080 coronary artery bypass surgery patients, to reduce cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. Record NCT00872001 is listed as terminated. None of the 5 records is about performance or exercise.
  • CBL-514 has more registrations than articles: 7 articles and 16 studies, all from the same company, Caliway. Of the 3 in phase 3, none has completed status — not yet recruiting, recruiting. It is development happening now, and still without a phase 3 result.
  • L-carnitine has a label, for something else. In the United States, levocarnitine is approved for primary systemic carnitine deficiency. In the Brazilian database of registered medicines, the search finds no active registration with the active ingredient.
