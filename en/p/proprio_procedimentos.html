Treatments that aren't a substance

Five procedures that compete with this site's vials and add up to 69,593 articles on PubMed and 5,349 registered studies

Partially approvedVerified against primary sources
!

Experimental material. Read before using anything from here.

Nothing on this page is a medical recommendation, prescription or treatment plan. It is an organized translation of protocols circulating in research communities and, where it exists, of what published trials tested. The two are marked differently — and they are not equivalent.

Most of the compounds here have no FDA approval for human use. Several are sold labeled "research use only", which means they have not gone through purity, sterility or dosage controls for human consumption. A community-reported dose is not a validated dose: it is what someone reported having done.

Talk to a licensed health professional before considering any of these compounds. If you already use one and feel anything unexpected, seek care — do not wait for the next routine test.

Specific note on this compound: A procedure isn't safer just for not being a molecule. Bariatric surgery is surgery, microbiota transplant is transfer of biological material between people, and a hyperbaric chamber has a defined clinical indication — the “anti-aging” use isn't one of them. A high count here measures the size of the field, not the strength of the evidence for the use the community discusses. The two lines where this matters most — hyperbaric and photobiomodulation — carry the caveat written in the query.

Summary

Not everything competing for the same goal as this site's compounds fits in a bottle. Bariatric surgery is the honest comparator for any GLP-1; fecal microbiota transplant, transcranial stimulation, photobiomodulation, and hyperbaric oxygen therapy show up in the same conversations about metabolism, cognition, and longevity. Combined, the five have 69,593 articles on PubMed and 5,349 registered studies on ClinicalTrials.gov. Volume doesn't make them good: it makes the comparison mandatory. Anyone discussing a peptide for weight loss without mentioning bariatric surgery is comparing against nothing.

Why a peptide site needs this page

Because the comparator changes the conclusion. A compound that takes off five kilos looks like a lot next to nothing and looks like little next to a surgery with decades of follow-up. This site spends the whole time measuring the distance between promise and evidence; without the procedures, the ruler falls short on one side.

Two method caveats. The search for hyperbaric oxygen therapy is crossed with aging on purpose: without that, the number would be for the established clinical indication, which is a different matter. And photobiomodulation was searched by its plain name — the number includes dentistry, pain, and wound healing, and is a ceiling for the cognitive use that shows up in forums.

The first three lines were searched by the procedure's name, without cross-referencing, because the name already delimits the topic.

No number on this page came from the site's secondary source.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • Bariatric surgery alone has 36,916 articles and 2,039 registered studies. It's the comparison baseline that almost never shows up next to the bottles.
  • Microbiota transplant has 644 registered studies and 61 in phase 3. The indication with consolidated evidence is recurrent intestinal infection; the records I opened include irritable bowel syndrome and COVID-19. Large field, target still scattered.
  • Hyperbaric oxygen crossed with aging has 168 articles and 2 registered studies. The only phase 3 trial the search returns has 30 participants and measures epigenetic age. That's the real size of the evidence behind a promise sold as established.
  • tDCS and photobiomodulation have volume and dispersion. 2,080 and 584 registered studies, spread across dozens of indications. A lot of small studies don't add up to one big study.
