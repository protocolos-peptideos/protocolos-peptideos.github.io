GnRH and gonadotropins — the analogues that have a label

Triptorelin, leuprorelin and goserelin have 86, 124 and 125 registered phase 3 trials, in cancer and fertility. Among the records from the five searches, none is about recovery after anabolic steroids

!

Experimental material. Read before using anything from here.

Nothing on this page is a medical recommendation, prescription or treatment plan. It is an organized translation of protocols circulating in research communities and, where it exists, of what published trials tested. The two are marked differently — and they are not equivalent.

Most of the compounds here have no FDA approval for human use. Several are sold labeled "research use only", which means they have not gone through purity, sterility or dosage controls for human consumption. A community-reported dose is not a validated dose: it is what someone reported having done.

Talk to a licensed health professional before considering any of these compounds. If you already use one and feel anything unexpected, seek care — do not wait for the next routine test.

Specific caution for this compound: A GnRH analogue in continuous use produces chemical castration: that is what it exists for. Using these active ingredients to "recover" the axis after anabolic steroids has no registered trial to support it, and the effect described in the label is the opposite. Menotropins and GnRH analogues are prescription drugs, with a defined indication and monitoring.

Summary

Five reproductive-axis hormones sold as research peptides: the GnRH analogues triptorelin, leuprorelin, goserelin and histrelin, and menotropins (hMG). Unlike most of this site, all five are drugs with a label. And the label contradicts the sales pitch: a continuous GnRH analogue shuts the axis down, it doesn't restart it — it is a prostate and breast cancer drug.

Why these five are together

This site already had kisspeptin, gonadorelin and hCG (on the muscle, bone and HPG axis page). Missing were the long-acting GnRH analogues — triptorelin, leuprorelin, goserelin and histrelin — and menotropins, the gonadotropin extracted from the urine of menopausal women. All five are in the library of a Brazilian peptide store, which I used as a list of what was missing here, and triptorelin appears there described as used in PCT to restore the hormonal axis after cycles. This site neither cites nor links suppliers.

The contrast is the subject of the page: all five are real drugs, with a label in the United States and, for four of them, active registration in Brazil. And the label says the opposite of the promise. A GnRH analogue given continuously does not switch the axis on — it switches it off. The histrelin implant label spells it out: after an initial stimulation phase, chronic administration desensitizes the pituitary and reduces steroid production in the ovary and the testis. That is why triptorelin, leuprorelin and goserelin are prostate and breast cancer drugs.

Two method caveats, before the numbers. One: the phase 3 counts for these analogues are large because they enter as background treatment in cancer trials — triptorelin's largest record is a Roche trial of another drug, giredestrant, in breast cancer. The number measures presence in protocols, not study of the analogue on its own. Two: the leuprorelin search uses both names, leuprolide (the name in the United States) and leuprorelin (the INN).

No number on this page came from the site's secondary source.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • No record tests recovery after anabolic steroids. I swept the title and conditions of the 1,419 records the five searches returned (the same trial can appear in more than one), looking for anabolic steroids, bodybuilding and post-cycle recovery. I found 0. The 15 that mention hypogonadism deal with the diagnosis of delayed puberty, congenital hypogonadism, castration in prostate cancer, contraception and pharmacokinetics.
  • Triptorelin has 86 registered phase 3 trials, and the US labels give two indications: advanced prostate cancer (TRELSTAR) and central precocious puberty from age 2 (TRIPTODUR). In both, the goal is to lower sex hormones. It is the same active ingredient offered to "restart" a man's axis.
  • Histrelin is the only one of the five without active registration in Brazil — 0 registrations. In the United States, the only application the search returns is a subcutaneous implant for central precocious puberty.
  • Menotropins is a fertility drug. 32 phase 3 trials, with infertility and assisted reproduction as the conditions in all the ones I looked at, and the US label indicates it to develop multiple follicles in an assisted reproduction cycle. In Brazil, 2 active registrations, in the gonadotropic hormone class.
