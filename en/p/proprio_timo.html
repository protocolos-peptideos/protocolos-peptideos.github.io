Thymus and Cardiogen — thymulin, thymopentin and the heart bioregulator

Thymulin has 886 articles and zero registered studies. Thymopentin has HIV registrations under the brand name Timunox. Cardiogen shares its name with a rubidium generator used in heart imaging

!

Experimental material. Read before using anything from here.

Nothing on this page is a medical recommendation, prescription or treatment plan. It is an organized translation of protocols circulating in research communities and, where it exists, of what published trials tested. The two are marked differently — and they are not equivalent.

Most of the compounds here have no FDA approval for human use. Several are sold labeled "research use only", which means they have not gone through purity, sterility or dosage controls for human consumption. A community-reported dose is not a validated dose: it is what someone reported having done.

Talk to a licensed health professional before considering any of these compounds. If you already use one and feel anything unexpected, seek care — do not wait for the next routine test.

Specific caution for this compound: None of these compounds has a product registered in Brazil. The registration the search finds is for thymostimulin, a different extract. What is bought under these names is sold as a research reagent, with no control over purity, sterility or dose.

Summary

Two thymic factors that were missing — thymulin and thymopentin (TP-5) — and Cardiogen, a cardiac bioregulator from the Khavinson school. Old molecules, lots of physiology, almost no trials, and none with a product registered in Brazil.

Thymic factors and the heart bioregulator

The site already has thymosin alpha-1, Thymalin and Khavinson's short bioregulators. Missing were two thymic factors — thymulin and thymopentin (TP-5) — and Cardiogen, the "heart" bioregulator from the same Russian school.

Two method caveats. One: cardiogen alone on PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov returns CardioGen-82, a rubidium-82 generator used in cardiac imaging, which has nothing to do with the peptide. I narrowed the search with AND (peptide OR Khavinson); the only record from the unfiltered search on ClinicalTrials.gov is a PET scan with CardioGen-82. Two: in the Brazilian database of registered medicines, the pattern TIMULIN finds one registration — but for thymostimulin, another thymus extract, whose name contains the same letters. I publish the finding with the caveat: there is no registration of thymulin.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • Thymulin has 886 articles and 0 registered studies. A lot of physiology of a thymic factor, and no trial registration in people.
  • Thymopentin once had a trade name — Timunox, in the title of HIV records. 7 of the 10 records are in HIV, all from the same sponsor and all with no start date given. The largest phase 3 is a Chinese trial in liver cancer, with unknown status.
  • Cardiogen has 9 articles and 0 studies — and only 4 of the articles are about the peptide. I read the titles: the 4 are tissue culture and rat studies, from 2006 to 2010, in a Russian gerontology journal and in an experimental biology bulletin. The others use the word in another sense, even with the filter. None is a trial in people.
