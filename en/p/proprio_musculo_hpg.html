Experimental material. Read before using anything from here.

Nothing on this page is a medical recommendation, prescription or treatment plan. It is an organized translation of protocols circulating in research communities and, where it exists, of what published trials tested. The two are marked differently — and they are not equivalent.

Most of the compounds here have no FDA approval for human use. Several are sold labeled "research use only", which means they have not gone through purity, sterility or dosage controls for human consumption. A community-reported dose is not a validated dose: it is what someone reported having done.

Talk to a licensed health professional before considering any of these compounds. If you already use one and feel anything unexpected, seek care — do not wait for the next routine test.

Specific note on this compound: Two lines need their own warning. Gonadorelin appears with 43,237 articles and 300 phase 3 trials — but the largest records I opened are for prostate cancer, because the search falls under the GnRH class descriptor. The number is a ceiling, not a measure of the use the community discusses. And enclomiphene has an inflated count for the same reason: part of the records the search returns are for clomiphene in ovulation induction, not for enclomiphene in hypogonadism.

Summary

Four blocks, one single question

The question is always the same on this site: what the trial tested, and what the bottle promises. In these fifteen lines, the answer is almost always that the trial tested a rare disease and the bottle promises body recomposition.

The cleanest case is afamelanotide: the phase 3 records the search returns are for erythropoietic protoporphyria, sponsored by Clinuvel — a rare skin disease with photosensitivity. Nothing to do with tanning, which is why melanotan I circulates. Setmelanotide's is the same: the records are for obesity from a defined genetic deficiency, sponsored by Rhythm, and one of them has fifteen participants.

Carbetocin is another example of the same type: the two largest phase 3 records I opened are for hyperphagia in Prader-Willi syndrome, sponsored by ACADIA — not for bonding, anxiety, or libido, which is how the oxytocin analog is usually sold.

Three search-breadth caveats are in the notice above and apply to gonadorelin and enclomiphene. The third is immunofan: I searched using the Latin spelling the literature uses, and the number is small enough that any spelling variation would change the order of magnitude.

Where the numbers don't add up

  • Afamelanotide has 6 phase 3 trials, all the ones I opened in erythropoietic protoporphyria. It's proof that a serious melanocortin clinical program exists — for a rare photosensitivity disease, not for pigmenting healthy skin.
  • Apitegromab is the anti-myostatin with a trial, and it's not for weight loss. 14 articles, 7 studies, 2 phase 3, sponsored by Scholar Rock. It's the same target that bimagrumab pursues by another route.
  • Bone is where the Brazilian registration is. Romosozumab (EVENITY) and vosoritide (VOXZOGO) have active registration; abaloparatide and palopegteriparatide, 0.
  • The immune side has two package inserts and two nothings. Efgartigimod (VYVGART) and tezepelumab (TEZSPIRE) have active registration. Low-dose IL-2 is a strategy, not a product — 57 registered studies, and the largest phase 3 trial I opened is for melanoma. Immunofan has 51 articles and 0 studies.
